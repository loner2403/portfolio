## Case-study copy (paste verbatim)

All five use `period: 'Bull AI · Sep 2025 to now'` and `role: 'Software Engineer, AI & Data Platforms'`.

### S-01 `research-agent-workflows`
**Problem:** Equity research means reading market data, filings and annual reports for many companies and turning them into analysis people can act on. Doing it by hand does not scale, and a free-form LLM answer cannot be trusted or audited.

**Built:**
- LangGraph workflows for Indian equities covering market analysis, financial projections and annual-report processing.
- Each run plans, retrieves evidence from a document corpus, analyses, synthesises and validates before anything is saved.
- Persistent state and run history, so every result can be traced back to the inputs and prompt versions that produced it.
- Clients for several LLM providers behind one interface.

**Decisions:**
- **Schema first:** Every model output is parsed against a schema. A run that fails validation is rejected instead of being passed downstream.
- **Cite or drop it:** Claims are grounded in retrieved sources, so an analyst can check where each statement came from.
- **Benchmark the providers:** Providers are compared on the same tasks, and point-in-time backtesting checks projections against what happened later.
- **Version the prompts:** Prompts are versioned like code, so a change in output can be tied to a change in prompt.

**Results:**
- 3 / workflow families / market analysis, projections and annual reports

### S-02 `document-extraction-pipeline`
**Problem:** Segment-level revenue for listed Indian companies sits inside PDFs such as annual reports, results filings and presentations, often as scanned tables and charts. It has to be extracted for thousands of companies every quarter, and it has to be right.

**Built:**
- A six-stage pipeline (retrieve, OCR, parse, discover, extract, validate) that works through about 3,000 listed companies across 18 to 19 sectors, one company at a time.
- It maintains roughly 5 to 7 years of quarterly segment-level revenue and related decision metrics for each company, fusing several data sources.
- The OCR backend underneath: self-hosted layout analysis turns each page into a semantic block graph, and only tables and charts go to a lightweight vision model.
- Extraction runs as an HTTP service with an engine pool, an admission queue, deadlines and graceful drain.

**Decisions:**
- **Send only what needs vision:** Layout analysis decides which regions are tables or charts. Prose never reaches the vision model, which keeps cost and latency down.
- **Cache by content:** OCR, vision and extraction results are content-addressed, so a rerun only redoes the steps whose inputs changed, and any run can be replayed exactly.
- **Treat prompts as contracts:** Prompt and schema changes are checked by contract tests, and evaluation harnesses with tracing compare every change against a hand-checked sample.
- **Repair numbers, then validate:** Strict output validation plus repair passes for periods, currency units and table totals. Cost and latency are recorded for every page.

**Results:**
- ~3,000 / listed companies covered / across 18 to 19 sectors
- 95-98% / extraction accuracy / against a hand-checked sample
- ~30 s / per page / end-to-end extraction time
- ~Rs 3,000 / per million pages / extraction cost

### S-03 `data-ingestion-services`
**Problem:** Every downstream system depends on an accurate master list of listed companies and links to their filings. Exchange data changes daily, and some of it is only reachable through slow, paginated web pages.

**Built:**
- A daily sync that downloads the latest company data from both Indian exchanges, shows what changed and then updates the master table in a relational store.
- A headless-browser scraper that walks paginated exchange search results to collect filing links.
- Market-cap rules that decide which companies stay in coverage.
- Scheduled, containerised jobs with checkpoints, so a long run can resume instead of starting over.

**Decisions:**
- **Diff before write:** The sync computes and shows what changed before touching the database, so a bad upstream file cannot silently overwrite good data.
- **Plan for flaky pages:** The scraper restarts its browser after crashes, retries failed pages and saves a screenshot of every error for debugging.
- **Resume, do not restart:** Long jobs write checkpoints, so a failure picks up from the last good step.

**Results:** none (the section is hidden).

### S-04 `conversational-assistant`
**Problem:** Visitors to the company website wanted quick answers about Indian companies (what a company does, how its numbers look, what its documents say) in a chat, not in a research report.

**Built:**
- The backend of an India-focused financial copilot: company lookup, financial snapshots, document Q&A and chart-ready answers.
- A FastAPI service that streams answers to the client over server-sent events.
- Retrieval over company documents with vector search, with data in MongoDB.
- Integration into the company website chatbot.

**Decisions:**
- **Stream everything:** Answers stream over SSE, so a response starts to appear immediately instead of after the whole answer is ready.
- **Answer in structure:** Responses can carry chart-ready data, so the front end draws a chart instead of describing numbers in prose.
- **Ground in documents:** Document questions are answered from retrieved passages, not from the model's memory.

**Results:**
- ~9,000 / active users / through the company website chatbot

### S-05 `batch-analysis-jobs`
**Problem:** Some analysis does not need a chat. Every quarter there are earnings calls to summarise, competitors to compare and revenue charts to refresh, for many companies at once.

**Built:**
- Three batch workflows on Vertex AI and Gemini: earnings-call summaries, competitor analysis and revenue visualisation.
- Runs start on a schedule or from a webhook.
- Generated summaries and charts are written to storage for the product to serve.

**Decisions:**
- **Batch, not chat:** Jobs run offline in batches, which suits work with a natural schedule and lets results be checked before they are published.
- **Two kinds of trigger:** Schedulers cover the regular cadence, and webhooks start a job as soon as new input arrives.

**Results:**
- 3 / batch workflows / concall summaries, competitor analysis and revenue charts

## Review amendments (Sonnet design + engineering + content review, 2026-10-19; Codex still unauthenticated)
These override the specs above.
- **Copy:** absolutes are softened ("designed so", "caught before", "replayed from its stored artifacts"). Slogan-style titles become plain titles ("Grounded in sources", "Prompt changes are tested", "Resume after failure"). "thousands" becomes "about 3,000". The accuracy figure reads "95 to 98%".
- **Results move up:** they sit directly under the header (when present) so a hiring manager sees numbers first. The full narrative follows.
- **Accent colours:** case-page numerals, results and decision rules use the page's own card colour, not always card-1.
- **One link per card:** each card has a single `Read the case study` link (testid `case-link`). The title is not a second link.
- **Chunk preload:** the SystemPage chunk preloads on pointer-enter or focus of the link, to avoid a blank Suspense flash.
- **Route wipe:** the colour comes from navigation state, captured in the pathname effect (via a ref), not during render. If there is no state (reload or Back), it falls back to gold.
- **Back navigation:** browser Back (POP) restores the scroll position saved in sessionStorage for that path, so the reader returns to the card they left.
- **Overflow guards:** `min-w-0` on content columns and `overflow-x-auto` on the architecture box.
- **Meta description:** `truncate(summary, 155)`.
- **Tests:**
  - Card links use focus plus Enter, because a sticky later card can intercept clicks.
  - The palette test waits for the option and clicks it.
  - The colour test compares against the live card's computed background.
  - The no-JS test also checks title and canonical.
- **Owner must confirm before deploy:** the commercial figures (about Rs 3,000 per million pages, about 9,000 users) come from the owner's own resumes and are already public there. The owner still OKs them on the site.
